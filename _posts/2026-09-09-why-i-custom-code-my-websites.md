I used to think that for a static business website, the best choice would be a drag-and-drop, page-builder, no-code solution. What is the point of writing code manually if it is not an application? It seemed like overkill — a waste of time and resources for what essentially boils down to a digital business card.

After building multiple websites with the usual website builders, I noticed that these websites bring 0 value. Why am I saying that? Because I watched them fail. They launched, looked "fine", and then generated nothing. No calls, no emails, no sales. They were digital ghosts.

It was then I realized the problem wasn't the idea of a website, but the tool used to build it. The perceived convenience of a page builder is a trap: a trade-off where you sacrifice performance, control, and, most critically, results for a superficial sense of ease. This is why I will never go back, and why I now champion custom code for every business website, regardless of its static nature. If you want the practical build process, start with [how to create a website](/blog/how-to-create-a-website/).

Here is what I discovered:

## Performance: The Non-Negotiable Foundation

My journey with page builders was a constant battle against bloat. Every "convenient" widget, every pre-styled element, and every third-party plugin came with a payload of unnecessary JavaScript and CSS. The result was a slow, clunky user experience. I realized that nearly 50% of potential traffic is lost to slow load times.

With custom code, I took back control. I am now able to build websites that are optimized for a perfect 100/100 page speed score. This isn't just a vanity metric; it's a core business function. A fast site retains visitors, improves search engine rankings, and ensures that every dollar spent on advertising isn't wasted on visitors who bounce before the page even renders.

## ROI: From Cost Center to Revenue Driver

The most significant shift in my thinking was viewing a website not as a cost, but as an investment. A page builder site, costing a few hundred dollars a year in subscriptions, seems cheap. But it's an expense that brings zero return. A custom-coded site is built with one singular goal: to generate leads and sales.

When you position a website as an investment, the calculus changes. If a new client is worth more than $1,000 annually to a business, and a professionally built site can secure even one additional sale per month, that website pays for itself almost immediately. The value isn't in the code itself, but in the business outcomes it delivers.

## Integration: Freedom from the Plugin Trap

Relying on a page builder often means relying on its ecosystem of plugins for added functionality. This creates a dangerous dependency. Plugins can be poorly coded, conflict with each other, and, most critically, become massive security vulnerabilities. Updates can break your site, and you are always at the mercy of a third-party developer.

With a custom code approach, I can build a bespoke CMS that is perfectly tailored to the client's workflow. More importantly, I can integrate with any third-party service (CRMs, email marketing platforms, booking systems) through clean, reliable APIs. This provides seamless functionality without the bloat, security risks, and performance hits of an army of plugins.

## Scalability: Full Control and Future-Proofing

A page builder is a cage, no matter how pretty the bars are. You can only go as far as the platform's limitations allow. As a business grows, its website needs to evolve. Custom code provides full control. I own the codebase, the server, and the entire technology stack.

This means I can scale the site to handle massive traffic spikes, add complex new features as needed, and refactor the architecture for optimal performance without being constrained by a platform's roadmap. I'm not just building a website for today; I'm building a robust, scalable digital asset that will grow and adapt alongside the business for years to come.

## Wrapping up

My initial assumption was dead wrong. Building a business website with a page builder is like building a house with cardboard: it looks fine from a distance, but it crumbles under any real pressure. Custom code is the only way to build a house of bricks — strong, durable, and designed to provide lasting value. It is the only choice for businesses that are serious about their online success.
